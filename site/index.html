一How it works

You say one line and click once. That’s it.

Watch one request from start to finish. When it needs a key, it stops and waits for you.

“Claude Code” wants to use “Website server”
to run ./deploy.sh

~/my-website

It’s a demo. Try any button.

  1. You tell your AI:
    “Publish the website.”

  2. It looks through Ask Key’s catalog and finds the key it needs.
    You don’t have to tell it where the key is.

  3. To use it, the AI files a request:
    run ./deploy.sh with “Website server”.

  4. Ask Key asks you.
    Who wants which key, to run what. It’s all on one prompt.

  5. You allowed it.
    Ask Key hands the key straight to ./deploy.sh, not to the AI.

    You allowed it for 30 minutes.
    The key goes straight to ./deploy.sh. For the next 30 minutes it’s used without asking you again.

    You said no.
    ./deploy.sh never runs, and the key stays put.

    Nobody answered.
    The request expired after five minutes.

  6. The AI gets back one line: “Deployed”.
    The job is done, and the AI never held the key. Every use is in Access records.

    The AI gets back one line: “Denied”.
    Using a key always goes through you.

    The AI gets back one line: “Expired”.
    Using a key always goes through you.

二Permissions

Every key follows your rule.

Everyday keys pass, important ones ask you every time, and keys your AI should never touch stay hidden.

When a key needs asking, Ask Key shows who wants it, which key, and what for. Allow it once, or for 30 minutes; for those 30 minutes, every AI on the same Mac can use it without asking again.

Allow

Your AI uses it without asking, and every use is recorded.

Ask every timeDefault

Every use waits for your yes.

Hidden

Your AI can’t see this key, so it never asks for it.

四Limits

What it doesn’t promise.

Ask Key decides who gets a key, and when. A few things are outside its reach; know them before you rely on it.

The full security model
  1. 01
    Programs can pass keys on

    A program you approve can print, copy or forward the key it receives. You are approving that program, not just the AI that asked.

  2. 02
    Names are self-reported

    The requester and purpose in the prompt come from the AI. They help you read the request; they are not a verified identity.

  3. 03
    Other programs in your account

    Once a key reaches a program, other programs in the same Mac account may read it. Hidden doesn’t cover that path.

  4. 04
    Timed allow covers every AI

    “Allow for 30 minutes” applies to every AI on this Mac, not just the one that asked.

  5. 05
    No backup yet

    There is no backup or restore yet. Keep a copy of important keys somewhere else.
